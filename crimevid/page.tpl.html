How these were made

Decisions. The netra-ask dispatch watcher from main, in replay mode, with nothing posted anywhere. The person gate (YOLO11s, 1280 px, full frame plus 2×2 tiles) opens the judge. Every 2 s Jev scores a 4-frame strip for break-in, theft, tampering, trespass, vandalism, weapon, violence, menace and normal, and gives one subject box. The default policy turns that into evidence E and a verdict: none, watch, deploy or urgent. This is the same policy the jetson-cloud edge units run (judge.py is byte-identical, with no policy override). The first deploy or urgent strip is the drone launch. The subject is Jev's box snapped to a tracked YOLO person, and the caption is Jev's.

Cleaned sources. News graphics, app overlays, editor marks and camera cuts were cropped or trimmed from each source before any model ran (crimevid/prep.sh), so YOLO and Jev only see camera pixels. Each clip notes what was removed.

Video overlay. Cyan boxes are YOLO11s COCO person detections with ByteTrack ids, on every rendered frame at 12 fps. They are for display. Blue POLICE boxes are tracks Jev marked as a uniformed police officer or security guard (p ≥ 0.5). Jev is asked about each track's crop, and asked again when a clearly larger crop comes along. Police are never picked as the suspect. The red SUSPECT box is the incident's tracked subject. If that box lands on an officer, it moves to the last suspect or the nearest civilian. The HUD shows the newest strip's facts, E and verdict at the frame where that strip was taken. In a replay the judge runs in step with the video. Live, each strip adds Jev's round trip, about 0.3 s per strip according to the watcher's notes.

Crime windows come from our human labels of each clip (event-labels.json). "From crime start" is the deploy time minus the start of that window. The clips are public YouTube CCTV uploads (news and police releases) plus MEVA outdoor CCTV for the benign clips. Code: Netra-Internal/sauron-hackathon, branch hack-crimevid, folder crimevid/.
